Biological Recovery Engine

How your body safely regenerates vital components after voluntary donation, backed by clinical hematology.

Whole Blood Donation

350ml / 450ml

Standard whole blood donation draws red cells, white cells, platelets, and plasma simultaneously. Enforced cooldown ensures complete bone marrow erythropoiesis.

  • 90 Days (Men): Replenishes 200–250mg iron deficit
  • 120 Days (Women): Compensates for monthly menstrual iron losses
  • 28 Days for SDP: Mandatory clinical buffer before platelet apheresis

Platelets (SDP Apheresis)

Single Donor Platelets

An apheresis machine separates platelets and plasma while returning red blood cells and white blood cells directly back to your circulation.

  • 48h to 7d Cooldown: Platelets replenish naturally within days
  • Zero Red Cell Loss: Avoids systemic anemia or fatigue
  • Max 24 Times/Year: Strictly regulated under NBTC guidelines

Biological Regeneration Milestones

Clear, jargon-free stages of what happens inside your body following whole blood donation:

Fluids & Plasma Volume Restored Day 02
Electrolytes and vascular blood volume normalize naturally through simple oral hydration within 24 to 48 hours.
Platelets Rebuilt Day 07
Bone marrow megakaryocytes rapidly produce new platelets, returning your clotting count to baseline within a week.
Platelet Window (SDP Eligibility) Day 28
Official NBTC clinical 4-week guidance window post-whole-blood before a donor can safely participate in SDP apheresis.
Red Cells Rebuilding (~75% Replenished) Day 45
Bone marrow erythropoiesis reaches ~75% replenishment. Reticulocytes mature steadily into full oxygen-carrying red blood cells.
Ready to Donate Again (Statutory Reset) Day 90 (M) / 120 (F)
Hemoglobin & ferritin iron stores are fully replenished (90 days for Men, 120 days for Women). Verified on site at licensed hospital blood banks before any new donation.

Who Can Donate Blood & Platelets?

Official safety standards from the National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO) and DGHS for voluntary whole blood and Single Donor Platelet (SDP) apheresis.

Age: 18 – 65 Years

Donors should be between 18 and 65 years old and feeling healthy on the day of donation.

Weight: 45 kg (50 kg for SDP)

Minimum 45 kg for whole blood donation and 50 kg for Single Donor Platelet (SDP) apheresis.

NBTC Cooldown Intervals

90 days for Men • 120 days for Women (Whole Blood). Platelet (SDP) donors can safely donate every 48 hours to 2 weeks (up to 24 times/year).

Hb > 12.5 & Platelets > 1.5L

Hemoglobin > 12.5 g/dL. For platelet donation, pre-donation platelet count of > 150,000/µL is confirmed.

Hospital Blood Bank Testing

Every unit undergoes mandatory ELISA/NAT screening for HIV, Hep B/C, Syphilis, and Malaria before cross-matching & transfusion.

Clinical Truths & Common Fears

Real medical facts to clear common doubts and misconceptions before your first voluntary donation.

Will donating blood hurt or make me weak?

You only feel a brief pinch for 2 seconds during needle insertion. The blood draw takes just 8 to 10 minutes. Afterwards, you rest for 10 minutes with juice and light snacks. Most donors resume regular office work and non-strenuous daily activities on the same day.

Can I contract any infection or disease?

Zero risk. It is medically impossible to catch any infection by donating blood. Every needle, collection kit, and bag is sterile, single-use, hermetically sealed, and opened in front of you, then safely incinerated in biohazard waste.

How much blood is taken and how fast does it refill?

Only 350 ml or 450 ml is taken, which is less than 8% of your body's total blood volume. Your circulatory fluid is replaced within 24 to 48 hours simply through regular water intake, and bone marrow replenishes red cells steadily over the coming weeks.

Do I receive a health checkup before donating?

Yes, free of cost. A qualified medical officer checks your blood pressure, pulse, temperature, and performs a micro-prick test to ensure your hemoglobin is above 12.5 g/dL before you are cleared to donate.

HEMATOLOGICAL REGENERATION

Biological Recovery Engine

How your body safely regenerates vital cellular components following voluntary donation, backed by clinical hematology.

Whole Blood Donation

350ml / 450ml Cellular Draw

Standard whole blood donation draws red cells, white cells, platelets, and plasma simultaneously. Enforced biological cooldown ensures complete bone marrow erythropoiesis.

MALE COOLDOWN 90 Days: Full replenishment of 200–250mg iron reserve
FEMALE COOLDOWN 120 Days: Clinical compensation for menstrual iron losses
SDP BUFFER 28 Days: Mandatory clinical buffer before platelet apheresis

Platelets (SDP Apheresis)

Single Donor Apheresis

An apheresis machine centrifugally separates platelets and plasma while returning red blood cells and white blood cells directly back into your circulatory system.

RAPID REBOUND 48h to 7 Days: Platelets replenish naturally within days
RED CELL SAFETY Zero Red Cell Loss: Completely prevents fatigue or anemia
FREQUENCY CEILING Max 24 Times/Year: Strictly regulated under NBTC guidelines

CLINICAL QUALIFICATION MATRIX

Who Can Donate Blood & Platelets?

Official statutory standards mandated by the National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO) and DGHS for voluntary donation and Single Donor Platelet (SDP) apheresis.

CRITERION 01

Age Qualification

18 – 65 Years

Donors must be between 18 and 65 years old and feeling healthy on the day of donation to protect cardiovascular reserve.

CRITERION 02

Weight Threshold

45 kg (50 kg for SDP)

Ensures that the total blood collection volume strictly does not exceed 10% of the donor's total hemodynamic circulating volume.

CRITERION 03

Statutory Cooldown

90d Men • 120d Women

Statutory cooldown for whole blood. Platelet (SDP) donors can safely donate every 48 hours to 2 weeks (up to 24 times per calendar year).

CRITERION 04

Hematology Levels

Hb > 12.5 • Platelets > 1.5L

Mandatory on-site hemoglobin micro-prick test. Platelet count > 150,000/µL is confirmed before connecting to an apheresis centrifuge.

CRITERION 05

Hospital Blood Bank Testing

100% ELISA / NAT Screened

Every unit undergoes mandatory screening for HIV, Hepatitis B & C, Syphilis, and Malaria before cross-matching and transfusion.

CRITERION 06

Statutory Legal Framework

100% Unpaid & Voluntary

Commercial blood trading is strictly prohibited under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Supreme Court of India directives.

CLINICAL FACTS & FEAR BUSTERS

Common Fears & Clinical Truths

Real medical facts to clear common doubts and misconceptions before your first voluntary donation.

01

Will donating blood hurt or make me weak?

You only feel a brief pinch for 2 seconds during needle insertion. The actual draw takes just 8 to 10 minutes. Afterwards, you rest for 10 minutes with fluids and light snacks. Most voluntary donors resume regular office work and non-strenuous daily activities on the same day.

02

Can I contract any infection or disease by donating?

Zero risk. It is medically impossible to catch any infection by donating blood. Every needle, collection kit, and bag is sterile, single-use, hermetically sealed, and opened in front of you, then incinerated in biohazard waste immediately following draw.

03

How much blood is taken and how quickly does it replenish?

Only 350 ml or 450 ml is collected, which is less than 8% of your body's total blood volume. Your circulatory fluid volume is replaced within 24 to 48 hours through regular water intake, and bone marrow replenishes red cells steadily over the coming weeks.

04

Do I receive a free medical checkup before donating?

Yes, free of cost. A qualified medical officer checks your blood pressure, pulse, temperature, and performs a micro-prick test to ensure your hemoglobin is above 12.5 g/dL before you are cleared to donate.
